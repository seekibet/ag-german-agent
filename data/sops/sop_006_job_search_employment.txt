SOP-006: JOB SEARCH & EMPLOYMENT IN GERMANY
Version: 1.5
Last Updated: 2025-06-01
Department(s): Advisory Services / Career Development
Related Partners: socialbee, IFB, WTS, Finance in Motion, BASF, Knauf,
                  EnBW, 360° Labour Mobility Programme (GAaNF)

================================================================================
PURPOSE
================================================================================
Guide Kenyans and Africans in the diaspora through finding employment in Germany,
understanding the German job market context, navigating application processes,
and accessing integration and upskilling resources.

================================================================================
SCOPE
================================================================================
INCLUDED:
- Job search strategies for the German market
- Germany–Kenya Labour Mobility Agreement (September 2024)
- 360° Labour Mobility Programme for skilled migration
- Integration pathway via socialbee
- ERP/SAP skills development (erp4students)
- Current and recent job openings featured by AG-Career Hub
- Self-employment and freelancing (overview; see SOP-005 for detail)

EXCLUDED:
- Detailed immigration / work permit procedures (contact Ausländerbehörde)
- Salary negotiation specifics
- Employment law (Arbeitsrecht) — advise users to contact a Fachanwalt für
  Arbeitsrecht (employment law specialist)

================================================================================
PREREQUISITES
================================================================================
P-1: Applicants need a valid residence permit allowing work in Germany (or
     valid work permit / EU citizenship)
P-2: For most professional roles: University degree or vocational qualification
     recognised in Germany (see SOP-008 if foreign qualification)
P-3: Language requirements vary significantly by role (see SOP-009)
P-4: For skilled worker immigration from Kenya: Adherence to the
     Germany-Kenya Labour Mobility Agreement procedures (new Sept 2024)

================================================================================
PROCEDURE
================================================================================

STEP 1 — UNDERSTAND THE GERMAN LABOUR MARKET CONTEXT
  1a. STRUCTURAL DEMAND:
      Germany is Europe's largest economy and faces a significant LABOUR SHORTAGE
      across multiple sectors. Priority shortage areas include:
      - Healthcare (nursing, medicine, care — see SOP-002)
      - Engineering (mechanical, electrical, mechatronics, software)
      - Information Technology (SAP, cloud, AI, software development)
      - Sciences (chemistry, biology, physics — e.g., BASF, Bayer)
      - Logistics and supply chain
      - Finance and tax consulting

  1b. FORMAL RECOGNITION:
      The new Skilled Immigration Act (Fachkräfteeinwanderungsgesetz), discussed
      at the 4th AG-Career Hub Career & Business Networking Meet-up 2024, has
      expanded pathways for qualified workers from outside the EU:
      - Recognises qualifications faster and more broadly
      - Introduces a "Chancekarte" (opportunity card) for job seekers
      - Allows experience-based (not just degree-based) immigration

  1c. GERMANY-KENYA LABOUR MOBILITY AGREEMENT:
      - Signed: 13 September 2024 in Berlin
      - Kenya was the first African country invited as partner nation to the
        German President's Bürgerfest in Berlin at this occasion
      - Expected to create streamlined, win-win labour migration pathways
      - Focus: Not just facilitating migration to Germany but also job creation
        locally, outsourcing, and skills transfer initiatives

STEP 2 — ASSESS LANGUAGE AND SKILLS READINESS
  2a. LANGUAGE:
      - Most professional jobs in Germany require German at B2 or higher
      - English-speaking roles exist mainly in international companies, tech,
        finance, consulting; require advanced English proficiency
      - Bilingual (German + English) candidates have significantly higher
        employability — this is the target for most diaspora professionals
      - See SOP-009 for language pathways

  2b. ERP/SAP SKILLS (high demand):
      - Germany's industrial and commercial sector runs heavily on SAP ERP systems
      - erp4students (erp4students.de): University-accredited online SAP S/4HANA
        courses designed for students and graduates
        Courses: Practical, career-relevant, recognised by industry
        Certifications recognised by SAP and major employers
      - Brandenburg University of Applied Sciences partnership for academic credit
      - AG-Career Hub partner — available at the 5th Career & Business Meetup
      - Case study: Peter Dianga used erp4students → secured position in logistics
        in Bremen, Germany

  2c. CV OPTIMISATION (German CV format is specific):
      - German CV (Lebenslauf) format differs from international norms:
        photo required, reverse chronological, no photos in UK/US but mandatory here
      - Susanna Kis (Lead Recruiter, IT and manufacturing, career coach):
        Presented at AG-Career Hub's 5th Meet-up on "Optimizing Your German CV"
        — Follow her on LinkedIn for ongoing tips

    → Intercultural support
  - Also supports German companies in hiring diverse talent for the long term
  - AG-Career Hub partner: Osman Vatansever (Community & Relationship Manager)

    and secured a job at a top German company

  - Contact via: https://www.gaanf.org

================================================================================
EDGE CASES & EXCEPTIONS
================================================================================
EC-1: QUALIFIED PROFESSIONAL WITHOUT GERMAN LANGUAGE SKILLS
  → English-speaking roles exist but are a minority; main pathways:
    (a) Build German to B2 while working in an English-speaking role
    (b) Join socialbee qualification programme
    (c) Apply to 360° Labour Mobility Programme if in targeted sectors
  → See SOP-009 for language development pathway

EC-2: FOREIGN QUALIFICATION NOT RECOGNISED
  → Some professions in Germany have protected titles requiring recognised
    qualifications (e.g., Arzt, Ingenieur, Rechtsanwalt in some contexts)
  → See SOP-008 for degree recognition process
  → Angella Kerubo and Angella Onsomu cases are inspirational examples of
    finding alternative pathways

EC-3: APPLICANT WANTS TO WORK IN GERMANY WHILE STILL IN KENYA
  → Germany-Kenya Labour Mobility Agreement opens new pathways
  → Skilled Worker immigration: Apply for a qualified worker visa
  → CHANCEKARTE: Opportunity card for job seekers — allows entry to Germany
    to search for work (requirements: qualifications, language, financial means)
  → 360° Labour Mobility Programme provides support through this process
  → Contact GAANF: https://www.gaanf.org

EC-4: STUDENT WANTING PART-TIME WORK ALONGSIDE STUDIES
  → Student visa allows 120 full days (240 half days) of paid work per year
  → Many students work as Werkstudent (working student) up to 20 hrs/week
    during semester, full-time during semester breaks
  → Werkstudent jobs at Finance in Motion (see above) are a relevant example

EC-5: GRADUATE WITHOUT ERP/SAP SKILLS WANTING TO ENTER LOGISTICS/SUPPLY CHAIN
  → erp4students programme is specifically designed for this: online, affordable,
    university-accredited SAP S/4HANA certification
  → Peter Dianga career case study: DAAD scholarship → erp4students → logistics in Bremen
  → erp4students website: https://www.erp4students.de
